Course Overview

A complete Grade 6 Technology unit focused on learning how to use digital tools and create original work with Canva.

Overall Goal

Students learn to confidently use a professional digital creation tool. They move from basic navigation and file management to designing, editing images, applying design principles, branding, and publishing. The unit ends with a high-quality poster ready for classroom printing.

Tool Required

Canva Education (100% free for verified schools). Teachers sign up at education.canva.com. Students join via school email or class invite code. Full access to premium elements, background remover, Brand Kit, and Assign features.

Structure

1 account-setup lesson + 12 skill lessons × ~30 minutes each. Optional Challenges add 5–10 minutes. Four formal hand-in projects + one final print poster.

Tech Skills Students Will Learn

Digital Tool Fluency
Navigate Canva interface, create custom canvases, use toolbars, panels, and shortcuts
File & Asset Management
Make copies, organize designs, download PNG/PDF, use transparent backgrounds, share links
Image Editing
Upload photos, crop, apply filters, remove backgrounds, control transparency and layers
Design Principles
Typography, color theory, layout, hierarchy, white space, balance, and visual storytelling
Digital Creation & Publishing
Build logos, posters, infographics, social content, and presentations from blank canvas
Creative Problem-Solving
Follow constraints, iterate designs, give/receive feedback, and prepare work for real-world output (print)

Learning Goals

  • • Teachers create a class and generate an invite link/code for students.
  • • Students successfully join the class and log in with their school account.
  • • Everyone understands the difference between a regular Canva account and Canva Education.

Teacher Step-by-Step: Get Your Free Canva Education Account

  1. 1
    Go to the Education sign-up page
    Open a browser and go to canva.com/edu-signup or education.canva.com.
  2. 2
    Sign up or log in
    Prefer your school email address. You can also use Google, Microsoft, or Clever if your school uses those. If you already have a personal Canva account, log in with it and upgrade.
  3. 3
    Select “Teacher”
    Follow the prompts. Choose that you are a Teacher and select the type of school (elementary / primary, etc.).
  4. 4
    Enter school details
    Fill in your name, country (Canada), school name, address, and school website if asked.
  5. 5
    Verification
    • If Canva recognizes your school email domain → you often get instant access.
    • If not → upload a photo or scan of your teaching license (this is the main document needed in Canada). Click Submit.
    Verification usually takes 24–48 hours.
  6. 6
    Create your Class
    Once approved, go to your homepage → create a new Class for this Grade 6 tech group. Give it a clear name (e.g., “Grade 6 Tech – Canva 2026”).
  7. 7
    Invite students
    Inside the class, go to People / Invite. Generate a shareable invite link or class code. You can also import from Google Classroom. Display the code on the board for the first lesson.

Student Step-by-Step: Join the Class

The easiest and best way is to join with the invite your teacher gives you.

  1. 1
    Get the invite from your teacher
    Your teacher will give you either:
    • A clickable link, or
    • A class code, or
    • An email invitation.
  2. 2
    Open the link or go to canva.com
    Click the link your teacher shared, or go to canva.com and choose Sign up / Log in.
  3. 3
    Use your school email
    Sign up or log in with your school email address (the one your school gave you). This is the best option. You can also use Google if your school account is linked to Google.
  4. 4
    Join the class
    Enter the class code if asked, or accept the invitation. You should now see the class name and any folders or templates your teacher has shared.
  5. 5
    Check you have Education access
    Look for a small “Education” badge or notice that you can use premium elements for free. If you only see the free version, tell your teacher right away.

If you don’t have an invite yet

  1. Go to canva.com → Sign up.
  2. Enter your school email address.
  3. Select your school from the list (if it appears).
  4. Request to join the school team and wait for a teacher or admin to approve you.
  5. Or simply wait for your teacher’s class invite — that is the fastest method.
Common Issues

  • Already have a personal Canva account → log in with that account and upgrade/join the class.
  • Verification taking too long → teacher can contact Canva support or try again with teaching license.
  • Student under 13 → parent/guardian consent is required.

Teacher Notes (5–7 min setup)

  • Before class: Create a Class in Canva Education and generate a join code or share the link. Place a starter “Name Card Template” (blank 800×600) in the Class Folder.
  • Display the Canva homepage on the projector. Point out the left sidebar (Design, Elements, Text, Uploads, Tools) and the top toolbar.
  • Have students sign in with school email. Walk them through “Create a design → Custom size” once as a class.
  • Circulate while they work. Celebrate first successful downloads — this builds confidence early.
  • End-of-lesson share: 2–3 volunteers show their name cards on the big screen (30–60 seconds each).

Learning Goals

  • • Log into Canva Education and navigate the main interface confidently.
  • • Create a custom-sized design and add/edit text and simple elements.
  • • Download a design as PNG and share the link with the teacher.

Student Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. 1
    Open Canva & Sign In
    Go to canva.com (or the link your teacher shared). Click Log in and use your school email address. If this is your first time, complete the short student setup.
  2. 2
    Create a New Design
    Click the big purple Create a design button (top right or center). Choose Custom size. Type 800 for width and 600 for height (pixels). Click Create new design.
  3. 3
    Add Your Name (Big Heading)
    On the left sidebar click Text. Choose Add a heading. Type your full name. In the top toolbar change the font (try something bold like Poppins or Montserrat), size (make it large — 60–90 pt), and color. Drag the text box to the center or top half of the page.
  4. 4
    Add an Icon or Emoji
    Click Elements on the left. In the search box type something that represents you (soccer, pizza, book, game controller, music note…). Click an icon or emoji and drag it onto the page. Resize by pulling the corners. Place it near your name.
  5. 5
    Add a Fun Fact
    Click Text again → Add a little bit of body text. Type: “One fun fact about me: …” and finish the sentence. Make this text smaller than your name.
  6. 6
    Download or Share
    Click the Share button (top right) → Download → choose PNG (best quality). Or click Share → “Anyone with the link” and copy the link for your teacher. Save it in the class folder if your teacher asked you to.
Visual Support to Show

Screenshot of Canva homepage with arrows pointing to “Create a design”, the “Elements” tab, and the “Text” tab. Circle the top toolbar where font/size/color live.

Challenge (Optional)

Create a secret code version — replace some letters with matching emojis (e.g., “I ♡ ⚽ pizza!”). See if a friend can decode it in 10 seconds.

Teacher Notes

  • Demonstrate the difference between a flat icon and a layered + shadowed version live on the projector (takes 90 seconds and makes a big impact).
  • Show students how to hold Shift while resizing to keep proportions, and how to use the color picker on shapes.
  • Encourage “overlap and layer” — this is the first time most students discover depth.
  • Have a “mystery mood board” gallery walk at the end if time allows (students guess each other’s hobbies).

Learning Goals

  • • Use the Elements panel to find gradients, shapes, and icons.
  • • Create visual depth by overlapping, duplicating, and darkening elements for shadows.
  • • Group multiple items so they move together.

Student Step-by-Step

  1. 1
    Create the canvas — Create a design → Poster (A4) or Custom square (e.g. 1080×1080).
  2. 2
    Background gradient — Elements → search “gradient” → drag a soft gradient to fill the whole page. Stretch it to the edges.
  3. 3
    Add shapes — Elements → Shapes. Add 3–5 circles, squares, or lines. Change their colors using the color tile in the top toolbar. Place them as decorative frames or background accents.
  4. 4
    Hobby icons — Search for 5–8 icons that match your hobby (bike, controller, cat, paintbrush, basketball…). Drag them on. Overlap some of them creatively.
  5. 5
    Make a 3D shadow — Select one icon → Ctrl+D (or Cmd+D) to duplicate. Move the copy slightly down and right. Change its color to a darker shade or pure black and lower the transparency. Send it behind the original (right-click → Send to back). Instant depth!
  6. 6
    Group & Title — Select several items (Shift+click or drag a selection box) → right-click → Group. Add title text: “My [Hobby] Mood Board”.
Visual Support

Before/after comparison: flat icons sitting alone vs. layered icons with soft shadows and overlapping shapes.

Challenge

Make a “mystery mood board” — use only shapes & icons (zero text). Classmates must guess your hobby in 10 seconds.

Teacher Notes

  • Show two extreme examples side-by-side: one “font soup” (5+ fonts, rainbow colors) and one clean, professional pairing. Ask students which one they trust more.
  • Teach the “2–3 fonts maximum” rule explicitly. Most Grade 6 designers want to use every font they see — this lesson installs the discipline.
  • Demonstrate the Effects panel (Curve, Shadow, Lift, Neon) so students know where the fun lives.

Learning Goals

  • • Choose and pair fonts intentionally (maximum 2–3 per design).
  • • Apply text effects (shadow, curve, lift) for emphasis.
  • • Use a shape or color block behind text to create hierarchy and “pop”.

Student Step-by-Step

  1. 1
    Start a Poster — Create a design → Poster (or search “quote poster” templates for inspiration, then customize).
  2. 2
    Main Quote — Text → Add a heading. Type a short motivational quote (examples: “Dream Big!”, “Be Kind”, “You Got This”, or a class-chosen quote). Make the most important word huge (100+ pt).
  3. 3
    Font pairing — Choose one bold display font for the big word and a simpler, clean font for supporting words. Stick to 2–3 fonts total. Rule: Less is more — avoid “font soup”!
  4. 4
    Effects — Select the text → click Effects in the top toolbar. Try Shadow, Lift, or Curve. Don’t overdo it — one strong effect is better than five weak ones.
  5. 5
    Background block — Add a rectangle or rounded shape behind the text. Change its color so the text pops (dark text on light shape or light text on dark shape).
Visual Support

Side-by-side: messy multi-font chaos vs. clean paired fonts with one strong effect and a solid color block.

Challenge

Create two mini-versions of the same quote: one “loud & crazy” and one “quiet & elegant”. Class votes which mood fits the quote better.

Teacher Notes

  • Background Remover is the “wow” moment of the whole course for many students. Demonstrate it live with a clear subject photo.
  • Remind students about copyright: use Canva stock photos or their own photos. Do not upload random internet images of celebrities without permission.
  • Show the Layers panel (or right-click Send to back / Bring to front) so students understand stacking order.

Learning Goals

  • • Upload personal photos and search Canva stock photos.
  • • Use Edit image tools: Crop, Filters, Background Remover, Transparency.
  • • Layer images intentionally and create a dreamy collage effect.

Student Step-by-Step

  1. 1
    Canvas — Create a design → Instagram Post (1080×1080) or search “Collage” templates.
  2. 2
    Add photos — Uploads tab → upload one of your own photos (or use stock). Then go to Photos tab and search “beach”, “mountains”, “space”, “city night” etc. Add 3–5 images.
  3. 3
    Edit image — Click any photo → Edit image. Try Crop, Filters, and especially Background Remover (magic wand icon). This turns a regular photo into a cut-out you can place anywhere.
  4. 4
    Layer & arrange — Drag images so they overlap. Right-click → Send to back / Bring to front to control which image sits on top.
  5. 5
    Dreamy transparency — Select an image → Transparency slider (top toolbar) → lower it to 40–70% for a soft, layered look.
  6. 6
    Finish — Add fun text + stickers: “My Dream Spot 2026!” or whatever year you choose.

Challenge

Make it surreal — swap your head onto a funny animal or put yourself floating in space using multiple background removals.

Teacher Notes

  • Bring a simple printed color wheel or project one from Canva Elements. Talk about warm vs cool in 60 seconds.
  • Students should open their Lesson 3 design and use File → Make a copy (twice). This reinforces good digital habits.
  • At the end, do a quick gallery: “Which version feels happier? Which feels calmer?” Students start noticing emotional power of color.

Learning Goals

  • • Understand basic warm vs cool color families and their emotional associations.
  • • Apply a consistent color scheme across background, text, and accents.
  • • Create two deliberately different moods of the same design.

Student Step-by-Step

  1. 1
    Make copies — Open your Lesson 3 quote poster → File → Make a copy. Do this twice so you have two new versions to edit.
  2. 2
    Color wheel reference — Elements → search “color wheel” and place a simple kids-friendly wheel in the corner for reference (you can delete it later).
  3. 3
    Version 1 – Happy / Energetic — Change background + all text colors to warm colors only (red, orange, yellow, pink, coral). Make it feel exciting!
  4. 4
    Version 2 – Calm / Relaxed — On the second copy use only cool colors (blue, green, purple, mint, teal). Make it feel peaceful.
  5. 5
    Label them — Add a small note on each: “This version feels…” so you remember the mood you created.

Create a third “one-color mystery” version using only shades and tints of a single color. Classmates guess the emotion without any other hints.

  • Show 3–4 famous simple logos (Nike swoosh, Apple, Roblox, Minecraft) and ask “Why do these work even when tiny?”

Learning Goals

  • • Design a simple, memorable personal logo using initials + a symbol.
  • • Apply strong constraints (limited colors & fonts) to create professional results.
  • • Export with transparent background for reuse.

Student Step-by-Step

  1. 1
    Canvas — Create a design → Logo template or Custom size 500 × 500 px (square is perfect for logos).
  2. 2
    Initials — Add big text of your first + last name initials (e.g., “JM”). Choose a strong, bold font.
  3. 3
    Symbol — Elements → search a simple icon that represents you (star, controller, animal, book, lightning…). Keep it simple.
  4. 4
    Constraints — Use only 2–3 colors total and 1–2 fonts. Experiment with layouts: symbol inside the letter, next to it, or behind it.

Good (simple) vs bad (cluttered) logos; famous examples; step-by-step evolution of one logo from messy to clean.

Challenge

Design a logo for your dream pet or imaginary creature (dragon-cat, robot-dog…) using the exact same simple rules.

Teacher Notes

  • Turn on rulers & guides (View menu) on the projector so students see the grid. Explain the rule of thirds simply: “Imagine a tic-tac-toe board — put the important thing where the lines cross.”
  • White space is hard for kids — they want to fill every pixel. Explicitly praise designs that “breathe”.

Learning Goals

  • • Use rulers, guides, and the rule of thirds for balanced layouts.
  • • Create clear visual hierarchy (title → details → call-to-action).
  • • Intentionally leave white space so the design feels clean, not crowded.

Student Step-by-Step

  1. 1
    Canvas — Create a design → Instagram Post (1080 × 1080).
  2. 2
    Turn on guides — View → Show rulers & guides (or turn on the grid). These are your secret layout helpers.
  3. 3
    Background — Add a photo or solid color background.
  4. 4
    Rule of thirds — Place your main image or most important element slightly off-center, on one of the imaginary grid lines (not dead center).
  5. 5
    Hierarchy — Big title at the top, medium details in the middle, clear call-to-action (button or strong text) at the bottom.
  6. 6
    White space + logo — Leave breathing room. Place your personal logo small in one corner.

Challenge

On purpose make a “worst possible layout” version (crowded, misaligned, clashing colors). Write one sentence explaining why it is terrible.

Teacher Notes

  • Charts in Canva are under Elements → Charts. Students can edit the numbers easily.

Learning Goals

  • • Structure information visually using icons, charts, and flow.
  • • Create a tall infographic canvas and use hierarchy effectively.
  • • Communicate a clear story with minimal text (5–8 words per point).

Student Step-by-Step

  1. 1
    Tall canvas — Create a design → Infographic or Custom size 800 × 2000 px (tall is better for timelines).
  2. 2
    Title — “My Perfect School Day” (or “A Day in the Life of Me”).
  3. 3
    Charts — Elements → Charts → add a pie or bar chart. Edit the numbers to show how you spend your time (sleep, school, sports, gaming, family…).
  4. 4
    Icons + flow — Add icons next to each section (alarm clock, lunch tray, backpack, controller…). Use arrows or lines to show the timeline flow from morning to night.
  5. 5
    Short text — Keep every text block to 5–8 words maximum. Let the icons and layout do the talking.

4 Hand-in Projects

These four projects are formally submitted for assessment. Each builds directly on the lessons and has a clear rubric so students know exactly what “good” looks like.

Project 1 Due after Lesson 2

“All About Me” Name Card

Students create a polished name card (800×600) that includes their full name, one personal icon/emoji, and a fun fact. This is the first formal submission and establishes the “make a copy + download PNG” workflow.

Must include:
  • Full name as large heading
  • At least one relevant icon or emoji
  • One fun fact in smaller text
  • Downloaded as high-quality PNG
Simple Rubric (4 points)
  • 4 – Clear hierarchy, creative icon, neat layout, correct size
  • 3 – All required elements present, readable
  • 2 – Missing one element or hard to read
  • 1 – Incomplete or incorrect size
Project 2 Due after Lesson 6

Personal Logo

Students design a simple, memorable personal logo using their initials + one symbol. Strict constraints (2–3 colors, 1–2 fonts, transparent PNG) teach professional branding discipline. This logo is reused in later projects.

Must include:
  • Initials as primary text
  • One clear symbol that represents the student
  • Maximum 2–3 colors and 1–2 fonts
  • Transparent background PNG
Simple Rubric (4 points)
  • 4 – Clean, balanced, works small, transparent, memorable
  • 3 – All constraints followed, clear concept
  • 2 – Too many colors/fonts or cluttered
  • 1 – Missing transparency or incomplete
Project 3 Due after Lesson 8

“A Day in the Life” Infographic

Students create a tall infographic that visualizes a typical (or perfect) school day using icons, a chart, short text, and clear flow. This project checks information design skills and the ability to communicate with minimal words.

Must include:
  • Clear title
  • At least one chart (pie or bar)
  • Icons for major sections
  • Timeline or logical flow (arrows/lines)
  • Short text (5–8 words max per point)
Simple Rubric (4 points)
  • 4 – Excellent flow, clear chart, strong icons, easy to understand in 10 seconds
  • 3 – All required elements, readable hierarchy
  • 2 – Missing chart or weak flow
  • 1 – Mostly text list or incomplete
Project 4 Due after Lesson 11

Branded Social Media Pack

Students produce a consistent 3-piece set (square post + vertical story + thank-you graphic) that all use the same personal logo, colors, and fonts. This is the strongest test of branding discipline before the final poster.

Must include:
  • Three different formats (1080×1080, 1080×1920, and a third graphic)
  • Personal logo on every piece
  • Identical color palette and fonts across all three
  • Clear purpose for each piece
Simple Rubric (4 points)
  • 4 – Perfect consistency, professional look, logo used correctly
  • 3 – Strong consistency, all formats present
  • 2 – Inconsistent colors/fonts or missing logo on one piece
  • 1 – Pieces feel unrelated or incomplete
Culminating Project

Final Project: Print-Ready Showcase Poster

Students design one high-quality, large-format poster that celebrates their growth as a digital creator. The poster is submitted for classroom printing and public display.

What Students Create

A single, polished poster (recommended size: A3 / 297 × 420 mm or custom 11×17 inches) that includes:

  • Personal branding — their logo used prominently and correctly
  • Showcase of best work — 2–4 of their strongest designs arranged with intention
  • Strong hierarchy — clear title, supporting text, and breathing room
  • Print-ready export — high-resolution PDF or PNG (300 dpi preferred)
  • Short reflection — 1–2 sentences: “What I’m most proud of…”

Suggested Themes

  • • “My Design Journey 2026”
  • • “I Am a Digital Creator”
  • • “From Blank Page to Pro”
  • • A personal motto + visual evidence of growth

Teacher Printing Checklist

  1. 1. Students export as PDF Print or high-quality PNG (Share → Download → PDF Print or PNG).
  2. 2. Check that the design uses high-resolution images (no pixelation when zoomed).
  3. 3. Confirm logo and key text remain clear at print size.
  4. 4. Print on quality paper (matte or glossy photo paper looks best).
  5. 5. Display in the classroom, hallway, or school showcase for authentic audience impact.

Final Poster Rubric (8 points)

  • Design Quality (3 pts) — Hierarchy, balance, color harmony, white space
  • Branding (2 pts) — Logo used correctly and consistently
  • Content (2 pts) — Best work showcased + meaningful reflection
  • Print Readiness (1 pt) — Correct size, high resolution, clean export

Everything you need to run this Grade 6 Technology unit — set up the class, manage submissions, and support students as they learn to use and create with Canva.

1. Set Up Canva Education

  • • Go to education.canva.com and get verified as a teacher (school email helps).
  • • Your School Team is created automatically.
  • • Create a Class for this cohort and generate a join code or share link.
  • • Students join with their school accounts.

2. Class Folders & Templates

  • • Create a Class Folder for the bootcamp.
  • • Place starter templates (blank name card, logo canvas, etc.) inside so students can “Make a copy”.
  • • Use Brand Kit if you want consistent school colors available to everyone.

3. Assigning Work

  • • Use Canva’s Assign feature so each student gets their own copy.
  • • Add clear instructions inside the assignment.
  • • Students submit back into the Classwork inbox for easy review.
  • • Leave comments and digital stickers for feedback.

4. Assessment Tips

  • • Use the simple 4-point rubrics provided for the four hand-in projects.
  • • Final poster uses the 8-point rubric (design + branding + content + print readiness).
  • • Celebrate process as much as product — growth over perfection.

5. Classroom Management

  • • End every lesson with 5 minutes of free-create time — it builds joy and ownership.
  • • Fast finishers do the Challenge or help a peer.
  • • Keep a “Design of the Week” wall (digital or physical) for motivation.

6. Final Print Day

  • • Collect high-res PDFs or PNGs a few days early.
  • • Print on good paper; color accuracy is worth the extra cost for this showcase piece.
  • • Host a mini gallery walk or invite families/admin to see the work.

General Tips for Success

Always Make a Copy

File → Make a copy before experimenting. Protects original work and builds good digital habits.

Undo is Your Friend

Ctrl+Z (Windows) or Cmd+Z (Mac). Students should use it constantly without fear.

Duplicate Fast

Ctrl+D / Cmd+D duplicates any selected element. Essential for shadows and patterns.

Group Often

Select multiple items → right-click → Group. Moves complex designs as one unit.

Download as PNG

Share → Download → PNG is the best everyday quality. Use PDF Print only for the final poster.

Use Class Folders

Teachers share starter templates in class folders. Students always start from a clean, prepared file.

End every lesson with 5 minutes of free-create fun. That joy is what turns skill practice into lifelong creative confidence.
